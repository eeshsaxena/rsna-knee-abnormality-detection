# MAX-ACCURACY PIPELINE — RSNA Knee (medal target, public 0.93+)

## 0. The one thing that decides this: the label ceiling, not the backbone

Everything below is built around a hard fact. Only 58 studies have gold labels; the image model is trained on ~4349 report-derived labels whose current quality is macro-AUC 0.65 (rules) to 0.73 (Qwen) *measured against gold*. That is the binding constraint. A 0.93 public model exists because someone's label extraction is better than yours, not because their ViT is bigger (DINOv2-large scored 0.5 on the public Models tab: it overfit the gold set). So budget effort as: **~50% on labels, ~35% on noise-robust training + CV honesty, ~15% on backbone/ensemble/TTA.** The good news working in your favor: image classifiers trained on noisy labels *denoise* (they learn the true signal and average out random label error), so the image model's gold-58 AUC can exceed the label AUC it trained on. That is why 0.90 is reachable from 0.73-quality labels.

## 1. Backbone + adaptation

**Use three backbones with deliberately different adaptation methods (adaptation diversity is worth more than parameter count here):**

| Member | Backbone | Adaptation | Slice aggregator | Res | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (primary) | **DINOv3-B/16** (public HF weights, offline dataset) | **LoRA r=16, backbone frozen** (+ trainable patch-embed + last 2 blocks unfrozen optional) | query-based **attention MIL** | 384px | AnyMC3D result: frozen 2D FM + LoRA r8 + attention pool beat every 3D method with ~1.3M trainable params on the near-identical multi-view shoulder-MRI regime |
| B (diversity) | **ConvNeXt-S** (ImageNet) | **full fine-tune** | **BiLSTM** over slice order | 320px | CNNs are data-efficient at 5k-exam scale and less prone to the catastrophic gold overfit ViTs show; BiLSTM member decorrelates from MIL |
| C (diversity) | **EfficientNetV2-S** (ImageNet) | **full fine-tune** | attention MIL | 320px | RSNA-2024/2025 winner backbone; cheap, strong CNN diversity; doubles as the efficiency-track student seed |

**Do NOT full-FT the ViT.** DINOv3-B full-FT on 4349 noisy labels + n=58 eval overfits (the public 0.5 large-ViT result is your warning). LoRA + frozen trunk keeps trainable params ~1-3M. Full-FT is reserved for the two CNNs, which tolerate it.

**LoRA config:** r=16, alpha=32, dropout 0.1, on q/k/v/o + MLP of all blocks; head + attention-pool query + a fluid-sequence embedding are the other trainable params. lr 1e-3 for LoRA/head, cosine, wd 1e-2.

## 2. Slice aggregation + per-plane + per-sequence fusion

**Guaranteed-invariant on train (from series analysis): every study has all 3 planes; axial fat-sat is universal; fluid-sensitive == fat-suppression exactly (drop one). But the hidden test may violate this, so every stage must tolerate a missing plane / zero series.**

```
per series: sample K slices (K=16 train / 20 infer) by ImagePositionPatient order
            2.5D = 3 adjacent slices as RGB channels
  → 2D backbone → per-slice embedding e_i  (+ add a learned FLUID-BIT embedding to each token)
  → attention-MIL pool over the K slices  → series embedding
  → attention pool over the variable # series in that plane (mask-safe, tolerates 0)
     → PLANE embedding  (sag / cor / ax)
  → late fusion of the 3 plane embeddings:
       cross-attention with 12 LABEL QUERIES (one learned query per label)
       each plane contributes a label-conditioned weight
  → 12 sigmoid heads
```

**Per-sequence handling: do NOT build per-(plane×fluid) branches** (axial-FS0 exists in only 19% of studies, 4 of 6 buckets non-universal, would starve/mask). Instead pass fluid-sensitive as a **single learned bit-embedding** added to every slice token. This lets the label-query attention *learn* to route: fat-sat/fluid-sensitive series (FS1, universal on axial) carry effusion / synovitis / Baker's / marrow-edema; non-fat-sat (FS0, T1/PD) carry anatomy for ACL/MCL/meniscus/OA.

**Why label-query cross-attention over plane-concat:** the gold co-occurrence analysis shows two latent groups (chronic-degenerative: OA+meniscus+Baker's; acute-traumatic: ACL+contusion+fracture, and these two are mutually *exclusive* r up to -0.33). A per-label query lets a weak head (MCL, Lateral OA, Baker's, the 3 rarest) borrow signal from its correlated partner instead of learning in isolation from ~9-12 positives.

**Inference robustness (test may break invariants):** detect plane from DICOM (fallback: tiny 3-class plane classifier when headers are messy), route each series, zero-fill + mask any absent plane. Train-time augmentation must **randomly drop a whole plane/bucket** (p~0.15) even though train never shows it, so the masked path is exercised.

**Laterality: flip all left knees to right** (from DICOM `ImageLaterality`, fallback orientation heuristic) so medial/lateral compartments are spatially consistent. This is a cheap, proven win and it makes the medial/lateral labels learnable.

## 3. Label pipeline (the highest-leverage block)

**Step 1 — finish the Qwen full run** (in progress, will write `pseudo_labels_qwen.csv`). Then produce an *ensembled soft label* rather than trusting either extractor:

- Rules v0 = high-precision / low-recall (trust its positives).
- Qwen-3B = high-recall / low-precision (trust its negatives; over-calls).
- Combine per label into a **soft target in [0,1]**: agreement→{0 or 1}; rule-pos & Qwen-neg → ~0.7; Qwen-pos & rule-neg → ~0.4-0.5 (Qwen's known over-call); both neg → 0. This is a 2-model adjudication, the exact pattern the research brief flags as beating single-extractor labels.
- **Fix the PF-OA Qwen regression** (0.66→0.59) with a rule-based high-precision override on PF-OA specifically (route-by-field: LLM everywhere, rules override where LLM is known weak).

**Step 2 — upgrade extraction if budget allows (biggest single AUC lever left).** Qwen-3B is materially weak on the low-resource 33.9% (Turkish 546, Croatian 406, Greek 321, Bulgarian 220) and French (81) is *zero-gold-validated*. Two upgrades, in priority order:
1. **Qwen2.5-14B or -32B via vLLM on cloud** (temp 0, constrained-JSON grammar, per-label "summarize-with-condition-in-mind → label" prompt from the MICCAI-2024 recipe). English prompt on native-language text (proven to work multilingually; skip translate-then-extract). ~1-2 A100-hours for all 4407.
2. **Hand-label a stratified per-language QA set (≥15-20 studies each for tr/hr/el/bg + any fr)** before trusting the pseudo-labels globally. This is the only way to catch the non-English failure that gold-58 cannot see (only 2-6 gold per non-English language; French n=0).

**Step 3 — derive pos_weight from the pseudo-label distribution, NOT gold-58.** Gold-58 was cherry-picked for pathology (mean 4.14 positives/study); the true corpus is far more imbalanced (rule-v0: ACL 0.10, Fracture 0.05, Lateral OA 0.03 vs gold 0.41/0.31/0.19). Using gold prevalence would badly under-weight the true rare classes. Because the metric is threshold-free macro-AUC, prefer **mild focal (γ≈1.5-2) over aggressive pos_weight** (heavy weighting hurts calibration without improving ranking).

## 4. Label-noise-robust training (three stacked techniques, all proven in RSNA-2024)

1. **Two-step training.** (a) Plain BCE on soft pseudo-labels → maximize AUC. (b) Freeze backbone, retrain only the head + fusion with mild metric-weighted focal loss. Prevents the weighted/noisy loss from corrupting the representation.
2. **Confident-learning / OOF filtering.** After the first fold pass, get OOF predictions on all 4349; **down-weight (not hard-drop) studies where the prediction strongly disagrees with the pseudo-label** (e.g. weight = 1 - |pred - softlabel| clipped). Retrain. This removes the worst LLM hallucinations, concentrated in the low-resource languages.
3. **Pseudo-label the test set** in the final round (0.5·label + 0.5·self-pseudolabel loss mix), RSNA-2024 2nd-place trick, mild but free.

**Gold-58 usage — never train from scratch on it (n too small, rare labels 9-12 positives).** Use it *only* for: OOF early-stop, model selection, and an optional last-layer calibration. Report gold-58 as a fixed eval with **bootstrap 95% CIs** (MCL/LatOA/Baker's will have wide CIs, quote them; do not trust a 3-decimal macro number on n=58).

**Augmentation:** rotation ±20°, shift, brightness/contrast/gamma, **CutMix p=1.0** (the single biggest win in RSNA-2024 5th place), plane-drop p=0.15. **NO label smoothing** (negative result there). hflip is **dangerous** here (it swaps medial↔lateral): only use hflip TTA/aug if you also swap the medial/lateral label pairs in the prediction, otherwise skip it.

## 5. Cross-validation (trust this, not public LB)

**No native group key exists** (all StudyInstanceUIDs share the pydicom anonymization root; no patient/site/date/scanner). Use proxies:
- **StratifiedGroupKFold grouped by detected report language** (`_reports_lang.csv`, en 39% / es 15% / tr 12% / hr 9% / el 7% / de 6% / bg 5% / nl 3.5% / fr 2%). Language ≈ institution ≈ the domain shift the private test probes. **Language is a CV tool only, never a model feature (absent at test time).**
- Add **one held-out-language OOD fold** (e.g. hold out Croatian) to estimate generalization to an unseen site. Random K-fold inflates AUC ~0.05 via scanner memorization: do not use it.
- Final selection by **grouped-OOF + gold-58**, never public LB (RSNA comps shake up hard: 2024 lumbar public 0.43 → private 0.49 reorder). Keep one "safe" and one "aggressive" final pick.

## 6. Ensemble + TTA

- **Train:** 5 folds × 3 backbones (A/B/C) × 2 seeds. Blend with **per-label logistic-regression stacking on OOF** (MRNet-style; lets each label pick its best plane/backbone mix). Group the 4 OA/meniscus labels and the effusion/synovitis pair when tuning blend weights (they co-fire).
- **TTA:** 9-rotation (small angles) + slice-resample. hflip only with medial/lateral label-swap. TTA is where you trade the 9h inference budget, so tune TTA count last.
- **Inference-time ensemble is budget-capped** (see §8): ship the 3 backbones but likely 1-2 folds each + moderate TTA, not the full 5×3×2×9.

## 7. Continued SSL on the 570 GB — worth it? **Skip the full run; do a light stretch only if a medal is in reach by W4.**

OrthoFoundation proves knee-domain DINOv3 SSL is SOTA and it is the single highest *ceiling* move. But: full self-distillation on millions of slices is A100-cluster-scale (hundreds to thousands of GPU-hours), does not fit a $100-300 / 6.5-week budget, and the *marginal* gain over public-DINOv3-B-LoRA is small relative to fixing the label ceiling. Verdict:
- **Primary plan: NO SSL.** Public DINOv3-B + LoRA captures most of the FM benefit. Spend the compute on labels + folds instead.
- **Stretch (only W4-5, only if grouped-OOF is already medal-adjacent):** a *short* DINOv2-style continued-pretrain on a **200-300k-slice subset** (not all 570 GB), ~50-100 A100-hours (~$75-150). This is the "if I have room" move, not the plan.

## 9. Inference-within-9h plan (T4, ~1300 test studies, internet off)

Budget breakdown (the decode, not the GPU, is the risk):
1. **Header scan + slice sampling (~5-10 min):** read all DICOM headers with `stop_before_pixels=True` across 4 vCPUs, sort by `ImagePositionPatient`, pick 20 slices/series. ~236k header reads is cheap.
2. **Pixel decode of sampled slices only (~20-35 min):** ~1300 × ~5.5 series × 20 ≈ 143k pixel decodes, multiprocessing over 4 cores, decode → 2.5D → preprocess → fp16 tensor. This is why you sample: never decode all ~819k-equivalent test slices.
3. **GPU inference (~3-4 h):** per study ~120 slice-forwards (3 planes × ~2 series × 20). 1300 × 120 = ~156k forwards. DINOv3-B/384 on T4 fp16 ≈ 150 img/s → ~17 min/pass. Budget **3 backbones × 2 folds × 3-TTA ≈ 18 passes ≈ 4.5h** at that rate, or trim to **3 backbones × 1 fold × 3-TTA ≈ 9 passes ≈ 2h** for safety margin.
4. **Blend + write submission.csv (seconds).**

**Total ~4-5 h with comfortable headroom under 9h.** Tuning knob if you run long: drop to 16 slices/series and 1 fold/backbone first (cheapest AUC-per-second), only then cut a backbone. Use `channels_last` + fp16 + batched-by-plane; skip torch.compile in the offline image if it is flaky.

**Efficiency track (optional dual submission):** member C (EfficientNetV2-S) distilled from the full ensemble, 16 slices/series, ONNX→TensorRT FP16. RSNA-2025 1st shipped exactly this. Cut slice count before shrinking the backbone. Only pursue if the accuracy track is locked.

## 10. Expected private AUC (honest)

- **Realistic well-executed band: private macro-AUC ~0.88-0.91.** The label ceiling (report-extraction quality) is the governor; image-model denoising lets you clear the raw 0.73 label AUC, and the public 0.906 DINOv2-small says ~0.90 is the neighborhood good labels reach.
- **Medal path (public 0.93+):** requires the label upgrade to actually land, specifically fixing the non-English 34% that gold-58 cannot validate. If Qwen-14B/32B + the per-language QA set + confident-learning lift effective label quality and the 3-backbone ensemble adds clean ranking signal, **0.91-0.93 is plausible but not guaranteed.** Do not bank on it from a single macro number: gold-58 has wide CIs on the 3 rare labels that swing macro-AUC.
- **Biggest risk to the estimate:** French (n=81, zero gold) and Croatian/Bulgarian mislabels concentrating error in specific labels, plus the private-test site-shift that grouped-language CV is designed to expose but only partially can.
