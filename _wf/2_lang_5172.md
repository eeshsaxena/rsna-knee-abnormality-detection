## (3) STRUCTURE & ANONYMIZATION
**Anonymization tokens are bracketed placeholders** — strip before extraction: `[DATE]`×759, `[TIME]`×359, `[REDACTED]`×110, `[ID]`×94, `[NAME]`×39, `[AGE]`×17, `[YEAR]`×14, plus `[MONTH] [GENDER] [PROFESSION] [AFFILIATION] [IDENTIFIER]`. A handful of stray brackets wrap real content (`[Segond fracture]`, `[grade 4]`) — do not blindly delete bracket contents.

**Two report regimes:**
- **Templated (colon-headed):** strong recurring section grammar per language. EN: `Exam Type / Exam Date and Time / Indication / Comparison / Technique / Findings / Cruciate Ligaments / Medial+Lateral Compartment / Patellofemoral / Extensor Mechanism / Osseous Structures / Impression`. ES: `Técnica / Antecedentes Clínicos / Hallazgos / Resultados / Impresión`. TR: `Tetkik protokolü / Bulgular`. These map cleanly to the 12 labels.
- **Freeform prose (≤1 colon): 1519/4407 (34%).** German and Croatian are almost entirely narrative (median 0 colons) — no section anchors at all.

## (4) IMPLICATIONS — rule vs LLM reliability, and the rare-language risk

**Rules (0.6534):** structurally capped. A keyword/regex extractor tuned on EN(+ES) covers at most ~55% of the corpus and collapses on **tr/hr/el/de/bg/nl (1908 reports, 43%)** unless per-language lexicons + the Cyrillic/Greek scripts are handled explicitly. It also degrades on the 34% freeform prose (esp. de/hr) where there are no section headers to anchor on. High-precision, but structurally low-recall by language.

**LLM (Qwen 0.7313):** multilingual coverage is its whole advantage here, but quality is **not uniform across languages**. Qwen2.5-3B is strong on en/es/de/fr/nl and materially weaker on the lower-resource **Turkish (546), Croatian (406), Greek (321), Bulgarian (220) = 1493 reports (33.9%)** — Croatian (South Slavic) is the biggest exposure. Expect Qwen's recall advantage to shrink most on exactly these, and its known low-precision / PF-OA regression may concentrate in the languages/prose it parses least confidently.

**The load-bearing caveat — gold-58 barely validates any of this per-language.** Gold-58 roughly mirrors the corpus (en 28, es 10, tr 6, hr 4, el 3, bg 3, de 2, nl 2, **fr 0**). So the 0.7313 headline is driven almost entirely by English+Spanish (38 of 58). Per-language extraction accuracy for the 60% non-English corpus rests on 2–6 studies each — statistically meaningless — and **French (81 reports, 1.8%) is completely unvalidated (0 gold labels).**

**Languages to worry about:**
- **French (81)** — zero gold coverage, and mixed EN-header/FR-body form that fools single-label detection; blind spot for both methods.
- **Croatian (406) & Bulgarian (220)** — low-resource for the LLM, freeform/Cyrillic, only 3–4 gold each.
- **Greek (321) & Turkish (546)** — sizeable slices (together 19.7%) that rules can't touch and the LLM handles least reliably, with only 3 and 6 gold studies validating them.

Recommended: hand-label a small stratified per-language QA set (≥15–20 studies each for tr/hr/el/bg, plus any fr) before trusting `pseudo_labels_qwen.csv` globally; and route by language — LLM for all, but keep rules as a high-precision override on the languages/fields where the LLM is known weak (e.g. PF OA).
